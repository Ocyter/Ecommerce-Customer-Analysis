ENGAGEMENT ≠ CONVERSIONDESCRIPTIVE ANALYSIS
THE BUSINESS QUESTION

Does cart activity actually tell us who will buy?

A generic funnel might stop at “customers add to cart before purchasing.” This analysis focuses on the customers who break that expected path.

THE EVIDENCE
401 purchased99 did not95 non-buyers had carts4 non-buyers had no carts37 buyers had zero carts5.17 avg cart items — buyers5.06 avg cart items — non-buyersSame 500-customer dataset
THE CONTRADICTION

Cart activity looks
busy. Not predictive.

Average cart additions are remarkably similar between purchasers and non-purchasers. That makes cart activity more useful as an engagement signal than a reliable purchase predictor in this dataset.

01

95 non-buyers had carts

Most customers who did not purchase still recorded at least one cart addition.

02

37 buyers had zero carts

Some purchasing customers did not follow the expected cart-before-purchase path in the recorded data.

03

5.17 vs 5.06

Average cart additions differ very little between purchasers and non-purchasers.

04

Do not overclaim

Without event timestamps or checkout steps, the data cannot explain why individual customers abandoned or converted.

WHAT TO DO NEXT

Find the missing
journey.

01

Instrument event-level sessions so cart additions can be ordered against product views, checkout starts and purchases.

02

Segment the 95 cart-active non-purchasers to identify whether they differ by device, location or browsing intensity.

03

Investigate the 37 zero-cart purchasers because their journey may bypass the simplified funnel entirely.

04

Do not treat cart count alone as a conversion score until richer behavioural data validates it.
