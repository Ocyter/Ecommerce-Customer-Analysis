# Case Study 03 — Insight Notes

## 1. Delhi leads on both headline measures

Delhi has the highest purchase rate (87%) and the highest average purchase count (2.73) in the sample. It is therefore the clearest candidate for understanding what a strong market looks like.

## 2. Pune exposes a metric conflict

Pune converts 81% of customers at least once, but averages only 2.14 purchases per customer. A purchase-rate-only dashboard would hide this lower purchase depth.

## 3. Ahmedabad is the reverse pattern

Ahmedabad has a lower purchase rate (77%) than Pune, but a higher average purchase count (2.45). Fewer customers purchase, but the average purchase volume among the full customer base is stronger.

## 4. Geographic ranking depends on the question

If the objective is first-purchase reach, Delhi and Kolkata are the strongest locations. If the objective is purchase depth, the ranking should use average purchases instead.

## 5. What should be measured next

To move from descriptive analysis to actionable market strategy, collect:

- Revenue and average order value
- Repeat-purchase timing
- Customer acquisition source
- Marketing spend by location
- Product/category mix
- New vs. returning customer status
- Population or addressable-market estimates

## Bottom line

There is no single “best location” without first defining what success means. This is a metric-design problem as much as it is a reporting problem.
