COMMERCIAL REPORTING / UK ONLINE RETAIL

How much do credits change the sales picture?

Positive invoice lines totalled £10.67m. After credits and other adjustments, the same file totalled £9.75m. A single number on a sales dashboard would hide that difference.

UK non-store retailer · 1 Dec 2010 to 9 Dec 2011 · 541,909 invoice lines

Decision: Show positive value, credits and other adjustments side by side. Prioritise the largest credits for reconciliation before drawing conclusions about customer returns.

Each line contributes quantity × unit price in sterling. The grouping preserves every line, including adjustments. The workbook keeps sub-penny precision through aggregation; displayed totals are rounded. This is invoice posting value, not profit, cash received or audited revenue.

02 / EXCEPTION REVIEW

A small number of large postings can move the KPI

One credit invoice totalled £168,469.60, or 18.8% of the credit value across the extract. A sensitivity check holding positive value fixed and removing only that credit lowers the overall ratio from 8.41% to 6.83%. That is a diagnostic, not a reason to delete a valid invoice.

03 / DATA QUALITY

Why all 541,909 lines stayed in scope

135,080 lines lack a customer ID. Dropping them would change a posting-level KPI that does not need customer identity. The workbook also contains 5,268 exact repeats beyond the first occurrence across all eight fields. There is no authoritative line ID, so the baseline retains them. Removing repeats changes the overall credit ratio to 8.40%.

Before acting: ask finance to match the highest-value credits to original invoices, posting reasons and settlement dates. Measure matched value and investigate unresolved exceptions. A C-coded credit is not evidence of a returned item, an unhappy customer or a lost sale. The monthly ratio compares postings in the same month, not matched order cohorts.
